## B. Routing Principle

The purpose of routing is to stop proposals from drifting into requirements by repetition.

For each item routed, the essential question is:

> **Is this a decision that only the Owner can make, or is it a team/agent framing that supports a decision?**

If the answer is the former, the item must be marked `OWNER REVIEW REQUIRED` and must not be treated as approved, pending, or implied merely because it has been written down.

If the answer is the latter, it may be preserved as working material, but its status must stay as interpretation or proposal, not as Owner authorization.

## E. What Each Proposal Would Change If Adopted

These are consequences for discussion, not commitments.

### E-01 — If F-01 is adopted

The working model would have an explicit way to describe source/event contributions across histories. Without it, the documents can still separate meaning by labels and status, but not by an explicit history-contribution convention.

### E-02 — If F-02 is adopted

Provenance language becomes more uniform, which reduces the chance of treating “referenced but not verified” as “verified” or treating “inaccessible” as “nonexistent.” Without it, the team relies on ad hoc phrasing.

### E-03 — If F-03 is adopted

The project would have a clearer rule for when similar words should and should not be treated as the same entity. Without it, term-sense discipline depends on convention and careful reading.

### E-04 — If F-04 is adopted

Authorization claims and verified authorization become easier to separate in records and summaries. Without it, the separation still exists in principle, especially through U-1 discipline, but without a standard claim status phrase.

### E-05 — If F-05 is adopted

External-condition evidence gets a defined scope of what it can and cannot establish. Without it, such evidence risks being over- or under-claimed depending on the summary.

### E-06 — If F-06 is adopted

The model would explicitly allow partial supersession as a possible authorization behavior. Without it, the model still contains a related change-discipline rule, but partial supersession remains an open validation question.

### E-07 — If F-07 is adopted

The team would have a fixed phrase for reporting relationships that are real but not yet verified as a linked chain. Without it, those relationships may be described informally and inconsistently.

In every case, adoption is a decision for the Owner. The current state is that none of these are established as approved or implemented.

## G. U-1 and U-3 — Do Not Close by Inference

U-1 and U-3 are intentionally left open. This task does not close them, and it does not propose a closure path that would pretend to resolve them without the actual authority or evidence.

### G-01 — U-1, in plain terms

U-1 is about whether specific Owner-approval claims can be independently verified against an accessible Owner source.

The claims involved include:
- G1-A/B/C/D/E and §2b records in `G-open-questions-decision-log.md`, which are agent-authored records stating Owner authorization and which carry the file’s own caveat.
- Owner-approval claims present in the verification files in `verification/`, including the Content Artifact v1 Status Ledger and the Gate 3 Owner Decision Ratification file, whose independent Owner-source verification remains open.

For routing purposes, the relevant question is not “does the claim look plausible?” but “which owner source, if any, can verify it, and who is authorized to confirm it?” This report does not answer that.

### G-02 — U-3, in plain terms

U-3 concerns preservation, integrity, completeness, and recoverability questions. This task does not establish repository-wide preservation and does not claim to.

For routing purposes, the relevant question is whether U-3 needs a separate evidence/follow-up path, and what authority governs that path. This report does not resolve that.

### G-03 — Why these stay open here

Closing U-1 or U-3 just to make the working model look more complete would violate the same principle this whole sequence is built on: writing something down is not the same as verifying it, and an agent summarizing a claim is not the same as Owner authorization.

If U-1 or U-3 are to be pursued, that should be a deliberate owner-authorized follow-up, not an accidental closure produced by another inspection task.
