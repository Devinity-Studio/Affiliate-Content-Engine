## 2. Findings

### IR-F-01 — Major — §5.1 vs §11 (final bullet): no lifecycle state for an approved, operating, not-yet-current configuration; supersession timing undefined

**Evidence.** §5.1 states: *Proposed / Candidate Configuration* — "No authority until approved"; *Superseded Baseline* — "Formerly Current; replaced by a newer approved baseline"; governing rule — "The Current Approved Baseline remains the authoritative approved reference until it is formally superseded, suspended, or retired" (singular). §11: "the running system may support multiple approved versions or configurations simultaneously — for example, several API versions, or old and new configuration during a migration — provided each version/configuration in operation is covered by an applicable approval and is recorded and controlled (which version is current, which are transitional, and when each will be retired)."

**Rationale.** During a migration, the new configuration is approved and operating, yet fits no §5.1 state: it is no longer "Proposed" (it has authority), it is not "Superseded" (nothing records that it replaced anything), and "Current" is defined as singular. The document also never defines *when* supersession takes effect relative to cutover (at approval, at cutover start, or at migration completion), so "old and new configuration during a migration" cannot be placed in the §5.1 state machine without guessing. This gap sits exactly at the junction of two corrections made by this same revision (correction 4: multi-version; correction 5: immutable content vs lifecycle events) and is the largest structural issue found.

**Recommendation.** Define supersession timing explicitly, and either (a) add a lifecycle state for approved-transitional configurations with entry/exit transitions and required authority, or (b) state that the old configuration remains "Current" until the supersession event while the approved new configuration is recorded as transitional under §11 — and reconcile §5.1's singular "Current" wording with §11's multi-version operation accordingly.

### IR-F-02 — Major — Revision History vs Identity Note vs §14(7) vs wrapper §5/§6: is R2 supplied or not?

**Evidence.** Revision History: "**R2 (historical draft, as supplied)** | Crouton draft supplied for this assignment — header read: 'Version: v0.1 (Draft), Date: 2026-11-08, Prepared by: Crouton.'". Identity Note: "That document is **not explicitly labeled "R2"** in its own header. … Accordingly, this revision cannot be confirmed as fully reconciled with R2 and with GLM v0.1, because **the distinct R2 and GLM v0.1 documents were not supplied**." Wrapper §5 question 1: "Supply the actual R2 document…". Wrapper §6: "Primary source received = Crouton draft, header 'Version: v0.1 (Draft)…'".

**Rationale.** One passage asserts the R2 text was supplied (as the Crouton draft, "as supplied"); three others assert the distinct R2 document was not supplied and ask that it be supplied. The intended meaning is most plausibly: *a v0.1-labeled draft was received and used as the primary source; it is presumed, but not confirmed, to be the historical R2; a separately labeled R2 and a distinct GLM v0.1 draft were never received.* As written, the passages point in opposite directions on a provenance fact that correction #6 of this very revision ("Source Identity Statement corrected to match documents actually received") was meant to settle.

**Recommendation.** Use one consistent formulation across Revision History, Identity Note, §14(7), and the wrapper, distinguishing three things: (i) the draft actually received (header v0.1, prepared by Crouton); (ii) its unconfirmed correspondence to the historical R2; (iii) the never-received distinct GLM v0.1 draft.

### IR-F-03 — Major — §6B: the classification rule leans on an undefined term ("impact") with no impact→level procedure

**Evidence.** §6B: "assessed on both dimensions: the scope of what is changed *and* the impact it produces. **Classification rule:** where a change affects multiple levels, the highest affected impact level governs — i.e., the change is classified at the level of its greatest impact, not its narrowest scope. A change that touches one component but impacts a subsystem is an L3 change, not an L1 change."

**Rationale.** The rule is directionally right (conservative escalation) but not decidable by a third party: "impact" is never defined, no procedure maps an assessed impact to a level, and impact is often only fully known after implementation validation while classification feeds §9A (pre-approval). The single worked example anchors the direction but not the measurement. Two competent actors can classify the same change differently — e.g., a contract-stable swap of a widely consumed component is L1 by scope and L3 by blast radius; the rule says L3, but nothing defines how much consumer impact makes it L3. Since the ladder drives proportionate assessment/validation depth (the exact subject of correction #2), the gap is material to the ladder's purpose.

**Recommendation.** Define impact dimensions (reuse §9A's inventory — contracts, data, behaviors, consumers — plus blast radius and reversibility), add a short decision procedure from assessed impact to level, and include two or three more worked classifications, including at least one boundary counter-example.

### IR-F-04 — Minor — §6B/§10: no explicit re-classification rule when later evidence shows greater impact

**Evidence.** §10 Rule 3: "If classification or authority is uncertain: stop the affected activity, preserve evidence, escalate per §6B Rule 2, and wait for explicit authorization."

**Rationale.** Discovery that a change's impact exceeds its classification is only implicitly covered (via "uncertain" → stop → escalate). The rule does not address re-classification mechanics or the fate of authorizations already sized to the lower level.

**Recommendation.** Add an explicit rule: discovery of higher impact stops the activity, triggers re-classification, and requires authorization to be re-confirmed at the new level before continuation.

### IR-F-05 — Minor — §6B/§4/§8: pure data changes and net-new additions fall outside the ladder

**Evidence.** §4 scope includes "data schemas, storage engines…"; §6B's scale column speaks only of component / subsystem / architecture / baseline; §6A strategies all presuppose an existing component.

**Rationale.** A data/schema change with no component replacement, and a net-new addition (nothing is retained/extended/replaced/rebuilt/retired), do not map cleanly onto the ladder or the strategy list. §8's data obligations clearly intend to bind everywhere, but the ladder does not say so.

**Recommendation.** State explicitly that §8 data-continuity obligations bind at every level, and either give classification guidance for data-only changes and additions or mark them out of the ladder's scope with a pointer to applicable governance.

### IR-F-06 — Minor — §11: traceability chain lists "validation planning" after "execution authorization"

**Evidence.** §11: "previous baseline → impact assessment → Owner decision → approval record → execution authorization → **validation planning** and implementation-validation evidence → new baseline."

**Rationale.** As a timeline reading, validation planning appears after execution authorization, while §2 and §9A require it before approval. The chain is a record list, not a timeline, but its ordering invites exactly the misreading that correction #1 was written to remove.

**Recommendation.** Reorder: "… → approval record → validation planning record → execution authorization → implementation-validation evidence → new baseline."

### IR-F-07 — Minor — §11 vs §5.1: "reactivate" unmapped; abandoned candidates have no disposition record

**Evidence.** §11: "Lifecycle transitions (supersede, suspend, retire, reactivate where permitted) are new records appended…"; §9C prototypes "may occur where separately permitted… it does not change the Current Approved Baseline".

**Rationale.** Reactivation is not mapped to §5.1 states (from which states, to which state, by what authority), and candidates/prototypes that are abandoned have no recorded disposition path — a gap against "Missing links are recorded as missing."

**Recommendation.** Add a transition table (allowed from→to pairs and the authority each requires), including reactivation and candidate withdrawal/disposition records.

### IR-F-08 — Minor — §11 (multi-version): approval granularity and retirement-date consequence undefined

**Evidence.** "provided each version/configuration in operation is covered by an applicable approval and is recorded and controlled (which version is current, which are transitional, and when each will be retired)."

**Rationale.** It is ambiguous whether one umbrella approval may cover the multi-version state as such (naming the versions) or each operating version needs its own approval record; and a recorded retirement date has no stated consequence when it passes.

**Recommendation.** State that the approval must explicitly name the versions/configurations in co-operation (one or several records), and treat an expired recorded retirement date as a governance exception requiring an explicit recorded extension.

### IR-F-09 — Minor — wrapper §3/§4 vs §6B Rule 2: escalation wording drift (claim-vs-text)

**Evidence.** Wrapper: "classification assigned to technically responsible actor; **escalation to Owner** only beyond delegated authority". §6B Rule 2: "escalated to **the appropriate authority** (including the Owner **where the applicable governance framework so provides**)."

**Rationale.** The wrapper summaries compress the escalation target to "the Owner"; the operative text escalates to the appropriate authority, of which the Owner is one framework-dependent possibility. Drift is confined to the summary layer.

**Recommendation.** Align the wrapper wording with §6B Rule 2 in any future reissue of the delivered package.

### IR-F-10 — Minor — §6B (L1): "agreed tolerance" has no stated referent

**Evidence.** L1 row: "Existing interface contract honored exactly; behavior within agreed tolerance."

**Rationale.** Who agreed the tolerance, and where it is recorded, is unspecified — an unverifiable criterion as written.

**Recommendation.** Anchor L1 tolerances to the applicable acceptance contract or baseline evidence so "agreed" has a verifiable referent.

### IR-F-11 — Informational — §3/§6B/§10: under-classification incentive has no independent check

**Evidence.** "the highest affected impact level governs"; §3 failure mode: "Authority erosion — technical actors treating their own judgment as approval."

**Rationale.** The rule creates an incentive to under-report impact (lower level → lighter minimum obligations). Counterweights exist (§10 Rule 3 stop-and-escalate; the §3 failure-mode list) but no independent check of the classification itself is required.

**Recommendation.** As a process minimum (not an authority grant): record the classification rationale and its impact evidence with the classification; consider independent review of classifications at L3+ under the applicable framework.

### IR-F-12 — Informational — §6B vs §9D: "Typical evidence" could be misread as mandating the old test method

**Evidence.** §6B L1 "Typical evidence: Regression evidence against the existing contract"; §9D: "The new implementation need not use the same tests, technologies, or internal mechanisms."

**Rationale.** The two are consistent (contract regression ≠ old test suite), but §6B does not foreclose the method-mandate misreading.

**Recommendation.** Add one clause to §6B noting that "Typical evidence" is non-exhaustive and method-neutral per §9D.
